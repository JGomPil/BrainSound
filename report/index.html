The paper

One article, four messages

Working title. Listening with eyes closed engages visual cortex, and imagined movement modulates alpha power: an EEG study of free listening to synthetic and natural sounds.

Data. 27 adult listeners, 27 sounds of about 50 s, eyes closed, 64-channel EEG, ratings and free-text descriptions of the evoked imagery after every sound.

Structure. Five figures, three tables, and a supplement. The sensor level is primary; the source level (template head, eLORETA, 68 regions) only confirms.

Supplement only: connectivity networks, graph metrics, signal complexity and the full multiverse. Kept for the thesis: the comparisons between imitations and recordings and the analyses of the free-text imagery beyond movement.

The case for the article

Rationale

Listening with the eyes closed is rarely experienced as sound alone: listeners report colours, shapes and movement, and sound designers work with these associations deliberately.

Cross-modal correspondences are well documented in the lab, and visual imagery engages early visual cortex. What is missing is how imagery relates to brain activity during free, uninstructed listening, and how much of it the sound sets versus what each listener adds.

Alpha is the natural window: it is high over posterior cortex with the eyes closed and drops when visual cortex is engaged. The alpha-inhibition account predicts the opposite for auditory attention (alpha should rise over visual cortex). A recent music study found lower alpha with more dynamic imagery and asked whether this reflects the music or the listener.

Gap

No study has combined free listening, non-musical sounds built to vary one property at a time, trial-level imagery reports, and models that separate the sound's contribution from the listener's.

Aims and hypotheses

Aim 1. Does free listening with the eyes closed engage visual cortex? H1: alpha falls relative to rest, more over visual than auditory sites (against the inhibition prediction).

Aim 2. How much of the experience is shared and how much personal? H2: sounds set reliable cross-modal correspondences at the group level, while single reports vary widely.

Aim 3. Does the imagery leave a trace in the EEG? H3: within listeners, more imagined movement goes with lower alpha, beyond affect, muscle activity and acoustics.

Aim 4. Which sound-design properties change brain activity? Exploratory: pitch, envelope, bandwidth and partials, each varied alone.

Honest framing needed: the study was not preregistered. H1 and H3 follow from prior literature; Aim 4 should be presented as exploratory.

What is new

Visual cortex is recruited, not suppressed, during absorbed listening.

The movement–alpha link holds within listeners with sound as a random factor, is specific to movement (not imagery richness) and rests on the personal part of the experience.

A sound-design result: the envelope, not pitch, bandwidth or partials, is what the brain registers, through the aperiodic spectrum.

Robustness to emphasise

Crossed random effects for listener and sound; within/between decomposition.

Bayesian model, 432-specification multiverse, leave-one-listener-out prediction.

Blind independent second coding of all 722 movement answers.

Source-level confirmation; specific control analyses for the envelope effect.

Limitations to state

27 listeners; one sound per level; fixed order within blocks; soundscapes always last.

Imagery reported after each sound: the relation is between trials, not moment to moment.

Template head for source localisation; correlational design.

How UVa coverage works. Through the CRUE–CSIC transformative agreements, the UVa library pays the APC in hybrid journals of Elsevier, Wiley, Springer Nature and ACS when the corresponding author signs with UVa affiliation and UVa e-mail. Requests are served in order of arrival while the annual quota lasts, and in past years only Q1–Q2 journals were eligible. Fully open-access (gold) journals are not covered, at most discounted. Confirm the 2026 conditions with the library before submitting.

Questions for the co-authors

  1. Target journal (see above) and order of authors; who is corresponding author (needs UVa affiliation for APC coverage).
  2. Methods details: amplifier, cap and reference; ethics committee and approval number; synthesis software and parameters.
  3. Questionnaire: written or spoken? Was the rest recorded with eyes closed?
  4. Framing of aims: confirm that H1 and H3 were expected in advance and Aim 4 is exploratory.
  5. How much of the imitation vs recording result to mention (one sentence in the paper, the rest in the thesis?).
  6. Data and code sharing at acceptance (code repository is ready, currently private).
